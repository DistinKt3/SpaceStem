# SpaceStem: Product Requirements Document

**Status:** Draft v0.1 (research complete, awaiting product owner input)
**Owner:** Justin McCain
**Last updated:** 2026-10-10

> This document is the front door to design. It locks the vision and principles, records what the research says, proposes a V1 scope, and lists every decision still needed from the owner (Section 14). Nothing below Section 14 should be designed or built until those are answered.

---

## 1. Vision

SpaceStem is a first-person space exploration game set in **our actual universe**. Where science has measured something, you see the measurement. Where science has only a best inference, you see a labeled inference. Where nothing is known, the game generates a plausible answer from real physics and tells you so.

You fly a ship with a real cockpit, travel from a planet's surface to other galaxies, gather resources to extend your reach, and come away knowing something true about space.

**One-line pitch:** *No Man's Sky's sense of wonder, built on the real cosmos, with honesty about what we know and don't know.*

**Why it can win:** Elite Dangerous proved a real-data galaxy works. No Man's Sky proved exploration alone can sustain a player base. Nobody has combined a *beautiful, game-grade* experience with a *visible epistemic layer* (measured / inferred / generated) that turns exploring into learning. That layer is our differentiator and must be present from V1.

## 2. Design Pillars

1. **Truth is visible.** Every object carries a provenance badge: **Measured**, **Inferred**, or **Generated**. Players can always ask "how do we know this?" and get a real answer with a citation.
2. **Wonder first, then understanding.** The first visual impression is awe. The learning arrives through curiosity, not lectures.
3. **Scale is felt, not skipped.** Distances and speeds are real; the game finds ways to make them dramatic and tolerable, never meaningless.
4. **Exploration is the reward.** Progress comes from knowledge and reach, not stat inflation (the Outer Wilds lesson).
5. **Seamless.** No loading screens between surface, orbit, system, and interstellar space (the Starfield lesson, inverted).
6. **Craftsmanship at launch.** A smaller, flawless V1 beats a broad, brittle one (the No Man's Sky launch lesson).
7. **Shareable.** Any discovery can be shared as a link or image that others can open and fly to, which is how we "show others how to explore it."

## 3. Lessons From Prior Games

Sources: web research for Elite and No Man's Sky (linked in Section 15); remaining entries are from general knowledge of these games and should be validated during design.

| Game | What to learn | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Elite Dangerous** | Hybrid model works: ~160,000 systems hand-placed from real catalogs, the remainder generated by Stellar Forge from stellar-formation science, with total galactic mass allocated between known and generated objects. Its real-data basis is its biggest brag. Criticism: vast but shallow on-planet content. | Adopt the hybrid and mass-budget approach. Invest in per-body *depth*, since "learn what makes each planet unique" is our core promise. |
| **No Man's Sky** | Launch overpromised and shipped early; trust took years to rebuild. A small team can sustain a living game by shipping steadily and listening. Procedural variety felt samey without strong rules for *why* things differ. | Promise less than we ship. Make generation *causally* driven (star type → planet type → climate → biome) so differences are explainable, which is also the educational hook. Don't announce features until they work. |
| **Outer Wilds** | Knowledge-based progression: nothing is gated except by what you understand. Hand-authored systems with strong cause-and-effect. | Use "Codex" knowledge and instrument unlocks, not XP grind. Real physics events (eclipses, tidal effects) can be designed moments. |
| **Starfield** | Fast travel plus loading screens broke the feeling of space. Many empty generated planets with little to do. | Seamless travel. Every generated body must offer at least one thing worth noticing (feature, anomaly, composition fact). |
| **SpaceEngine / Celestia / Universe Sandbox** | Prove real-data exploration and scientific visualization have a passionate audience. They are tools more than games: weak goals, loop, and onboarding. | Keep their scientific credibility, add a game loop and onboarding. |
| **Kerbal Space Program** | Players will learn real orbital mechanics if feedback is immediate and failure is funny. | Teach through play; keep physics honest but forgiving in V1. |
| **Eve Online / Star Citizen (cautionary)** | Scope creep and feature promises can consume years. | Strict V1 cut line (Section 9) with a written "not in V1" list. |

## 4. Audience

To be confirmed (Q1). Working assumption: curious adults and teens who love space and exploration games (No Man's Sky, Outer Wilds, Kerbal), plus educators and students who want a rigorous, explorable universe. Design must serve both without becoming either a toy or a textbook.

## 5. The Universe: Three Data Tiers

Everything in the world is one of:

| Tier | Meaning | Examples | Badge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Measured** | Directly observed and catalogued | Sun, planets and moons of our Solar System (JPL ephemerides), ~6,000+ confirmed exoplanets (count to verify live via archive), Gaia stars, known galaxies, supernova remnants, pulsars | Solid |
| **Inferred** | Not observed, but strongly constrained by science | Surface and atmosphere of an exoplanet from its mass, radius, stellar flux, and spectra; undetected planets around a star given known detection biases; dust distribution; dark-matter halo | Dashed |
| **Generated** | Plausible fill from physical rules where we have no data | Most stars beyond a few thousand light-years, most planets, surface terrain detail | Dotted |

**Rules:**
- Generation is seeded and deterministic: the same coordinates always give the same result for every player.
- Generation must respect physical constraints and the *measured statistics* of the real universe (stellar initial mass function, planet-occurrence rates from Kepler/TESS, metallicity gradient).
- When new data arrives (a planet discovered, a Gaia data release) a Generated object may be **promoted** to Inferred or Measured. The game should treat this as a feature ("this planet you visited was confirmed in 2027").
- The UI never shows a Generated or Inferred thing as fact. Terrain on an exoplanet is Inferred/Generated; we do not have photographs of it.

## 6. Data Sources (candidate list)

Licensing and terms must be verified per source before use (open item, Q11).

| Domain | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Stars (3D positions) | **ESA Gaia DR3** (~1.8 billion sources). **DR4 is scheduled for 2 Dec 2026** (first 5.5 years of data), which falls right inside our build window | Backbone of the Milky Way. Plan the pipeline to ingest DR4 when it lands. |
| Bright/named stars | Hipparcos, SIMBAD, HYG database | Names, constellations, familiar stars |
| Exoplanets | **NASA Exoplanet Archive** via TAP (tables `ps`, `pscomppars`) | Real planets and host stars |
| Solar System | **JPL Horizons / SPICE kernels**, NASA PDS, NASA Visible Planets and 3D Resources, USGS Astrogeology | Exact positions, textures, elevation (LOLA, MOLA, etc.) |
| Small bodies | JPL Small-Body Database, Minor Planet Center | Asteroids, comets, near-Earth objects |
| Galaxies and large scale | NED, SDSS, 2MASS, 6dF, DESI results; simulation data (IllustrisTNG) for structure | Local Group, nearby clusters, cosmic web |
| Deep sky | Messier/NGC/IC, Planck, WISE | Nebulae, clusters, CMB sky |
| Anomalies and transients | Pulsars (ATNF), magnetars, TNS (supernovae), GCN (gamma-ray bursts), LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA (GWOSC) events, Fast Radio Bursts (CHIME), Tabby's Star, Oumuamua, Boyajian-type oddities | The "anomalies from outer space" feature |
| Imagery | NASA/ESA/CSA media (JWST, Hubble), MAST | Real imagery inside the Codex and as sky/nebula textures where licensing allows |
| Science text | NASA, ESA, peer-reviewed papers | Codex entries with citations |

**Pipeline principle:** data is processed **offline** into compact, streamable tile formats (octree for 3D stars, HEALPix for sky maps, mesh/heightmap tiles for planets). The game never queries NASA at runtime for core play. An optional live-feed layer (new supernova alerts) is a post-V1 candidate.

## 7. Scale Ladder and Travel

The player moves through these scales without loading screens:

1. **Surface** (meters)
2. **Low orbit and atmosphere** (kilometers)
3. **System** (AU: planets, moons, belts)
4. **Local stellar neighborhood** (light-years)
5. **Galactic** (kiloparsecs)
6. **Intergalactic** (megaparsecs) and cosmic web

### Travel modes (proposed)

| Mode | Purpose | Speed class | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Atmospheric / thrusters** | Flight near surfaces | km/s | Fuel (small) |
| **Impulse** | Within a system | up to fractions of c; system crossing in minutes | Fuel |
| **Cruise** | Between planets with a "real time" display | ~0.1c to c | Fuel |
| **Hyperspeed** | Between stars; the sky streaks, real light-travel time shown on HUD (e.g. "4.2 ly") | Multiples of c, scales with upgrade | Hyper-fuel and cooldown |
| **Jump / Warp** | Star to far star, galactic, and intergalactic | Instant with a loading-free transition effect | Rare resource, charge time |

**Realism principle:** speeds above c are fiction, and we say so. The game's honesty rule is that *the universe is real, the drive is fictional*, and the Codex has an entry explaining the real physics (and the real cheats: Alcubierre metric, wormholes) next to it.

**Open design question (Q4):** how much "realistic friction" is fun, such as relativistic time dilation and the lookback-time effect (distant galaxies are seen as they were), versus how much is simply cosmetic.

## 8. Core Loop and Progression

**Moment to moment:** fly, scan, land, survey, collect.
**Session:** pick a target (curiosity, a mission, an anomaly) → travel → investigate → gather → upgrade or share.
**Long-term:** extend reach → see rarer things → fill the Codex.

### Resources (proposed, tied to real science)

- **Fuel:** hydrogen and helium scooped from stars and gas giants.
- **Minerals/elements:** actual elements from the periodic table, whose availability depends on body type (iron from metallic asteroids, helium-3 from gas giants, ice from comets).
- **Exotic:** neutron star or black-hole proximity materials for the biggest jumps (clearly labeled as game-fiction conversion of real exotic physics).
- **Data:** the most important "currency". Scans produce Codex entries and unlock instruments.

### Progression

- Ship systems: engine (speed), hyperdrive (range), scanner suite, cargo, shields (V1.5+), life support.
- **Instrument unlocks** (spectrometer, radar, magnetometer, gravimeter) that let you *learn new things* from the same object (Outer Wilds style: new knowledge, not just numbers).
- **Codex** records what you've personally observed, with real citations.

## 9. V1 Scope (proposal for discussion)

**Guiding rule:** V1 is *one* complete, flawless slice of the full vision, not a thin layer over everything.

### In V1

| Area | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Solar System** | All planets, major moons, Sun, and selected small bodies at real positions, using real elevation and texture data where licensed; seamless surface↔orbit↔system |
| **Local stars** | Real stars within a chosen radius (e.g. a few thousand ly) from Gaia/Hipparcos, with named-star fidelity |
| **Real exoplanets** | Several hundred to all confirmed ones, with Inferred surfaces and atmospheres derived from measured mass/radius/flux |
| **Generated space** | The Milky Way's remaining stars and planets via the hybrid generator (Section 5) |
| **Cockpit** | One ship, fully modelled cockpit with working instruments (speed, fuel, nav map, scanner) |
| **Travel** | Thrusters, impulse, cruise, hyperspeed, and one star-to-star jump |
| **Exploration** | Scan, land (at least on rocky bodies), simple on-foot or rover survey (to confirm, Q7) |
| **Resources** | Fuel plus a small set of elements; refuel and upgrade loop |
| **Anomalies** | A curated set (e.g. a pulsar, a supernova remnant, a black hole, a famous odd star) with correct science |
| **Codex** | Provenance badges, citations, discovery log |
| **Sharing** | Shareable coordinates/link and screenshot mode |
| **Onboarding** | First 10 minutes designed end to end |

### Not in V1 (explicit)

Weapons, missiles, death rays, combat, multiplayer, base building, trading economy, NPC factions, other galaxies as explorable interiors (they appear as real, distant, visitable-from-outside objects), live data feeds, VR.

### V1.5 / V2 candidates

Intergalactic exploration with generated interiors, the full 3D cosmic web, live transient alerts, ship customization, combat sandbox, co-op "expeditions", educator mode and classroom share, VR.

## 10. The Ship and Views

- **Cockpit (default):** full 3D interior, believable instruments, physical switches, head look; the HUD is *diegetic* where possible (readouts on glass and panels) so we avoid a flat overlay feel.
- **Views:** cockpit, external chase, free camera / photo mode, map views (system, local stars, galactic).
- **Controls:** keyboard and mouse, gamepad; HOTAS is a stretch.
- **Ship identity ("unique cockpit"):** needs art direction input (Q6): era, material, silhouette, how "real engineering" versus "sci-fi" it should feel.

## 11. Technical Direction (proposal, to validate in a prototype)

**Rendering:** three.js with **WebGPU as primary renderer and a WebGL2 fallback**. Note that three.js WebGPU support and its interaction with logarithmic depth buffers and reverse-Z are less documented than the WebGL path (research found only partial examples), so a **technical spike** is the first engineering milestone.

**Large-scale precision:**
- Floating origin (ship stays at origin, world re-based) to avoid float jitter. Known pitfall: rebasing must update positions without rebuilding the scene graph (one reported implementation fell to ~1 FPS from doing this through a React tree), so use plain three.js, not React Three Fiber, for the world.
- Double-precision world coordinates on the CPU; relative float coordinates on the GPU.
- Logarithmic or reverse-Z depth strategy, validated for WebGPU.

**Planets:** cube-sphere quadtree with continuous LOD, GPU or worker-generated heightfields, imagery plus procedural detail blended by scale.

**Stars and galaxies:** octree streamed in the background from pre-built tiles; impostors/billboards for distant objects; HEALPix sky texture for the background.

**Determinism:** all generation takes `(universe_seed, coordinates)` and is deterministic across machines (avoid non-deterministic floating-point paths in generation, or fix them in integers).

**Performance budgets (to confirm, Q8):** 60 fps on a mid-range laptop at 1080p; initial load under X seconds; incremental streaming.

**Data layer:** offline pipeline (Python or similar) building versioned tile packages, with a manifest recording the source dataset version for each, supporting "promotion" when sources update.

**Open platform question (Q2):** pure web (instant access, easy sharing, perf ceiling) versus native (higher fidelity, heavier distribution). This choice is the biggest cost driver in the project. A web client can be wrapped later (Electron/Tauri); a native one can't easily go to web.

## 12. Audio and Feel

Music: sparse and ambient. Sound design must honor "space is silent" with a considered choice (interior ship sound, instrument tones, and a documented rule about what you hear outside), which is another place to be honest and still beautiful. Needs an audio direction decision (Q9).

## 13. Success Metrics

- **Wonder:** median time to first "wow" screenshot shared.
- **Learning:** share of players who open a Codex entry or a "how do we know this?" panel; a short in-game quiz score or self-report.
- **Retention:** D1 / D7 / D30.
- **Quality:** crash-free sessions, frame-time percentiles on target hardware, zero z-fighting or precision-jitter bugs on a defined test route.
- **Scientific accuracy:** all Measured claims traceable to a source; an external science reviewer sign-off (Q10).

## 14. Open Questions for the Owner

The first four are the highest leverage and are being asked in chat. The rest need answers before design starts.

**Strategic**
1. **Audience and purpose.** Primary: entertainment, education, or both? Age range?
2. **Platform.** Web browser, desktop native, both? Mobile?
3. **Business model.** Free, paid, school licensing, open source? (Affects data licensing and architecture.)
4. **Realism versus fun in travel.** Include relativistic effects and lookback time, or purely visual speed?

**Scope and content**
5. **V1 slice.** Is the proposed V1 (Solar System + real local stars + exoplanets + generated Milky Way, one ship, no combat) the right first release? What is the single feature you'd refuse to ship without?
6. **Ship and art direction.** Realistic NASA-style engineering, retro-future, or sleek sci-fi? Any reference images?
7. **Landing and surface.** Ship-only, or on foot / rover? Does V1 need first-person surface exploration?
8. **Target hardware and performance floor.** Minimum spec you'd accept?
9. **Audio direction.** Realistic silence, scored ambient, or both?

**Operations**
10. **Science validation.** Do you have access to an astronomer or educator as an advisor or reviewer?
11. **Data licensing.** Comfortable with us verifying each source's terms before use, and dropping any source that doesn't fit?
12. **Team and timeline.** Who is building (you, a team, me), and what is the target date and budget? Gaia DR4 lands 2 Dec 2026, which could be a launch-narrative moment if the timeline allows.
13. **Name and brand.** Is "SpaceStem" final?
14. **Narrative.** Pure sandbox, or a light guiding thread (e.g. a mission to understand where we are)?
15. **Accessibility and localization.** Colorblind modes, subtitles, languages in V1?

## 15. Next Steps (after your answers)

Design deliverables, in order:

1. **Technical spike:** WebGPU floating origin + depth + planet LOD proof (decides platform feasibility).
2. **Data feasibility:** build one end-to-end slice (Sol + 1,000 Gaia stars + 10 exoplanets) from raw archive to screen.
3. **Game design document:** loops, resources, progression, tuning tables.
4. **Cockpit and ship design brief:** art direction, instrument list, control scheme.
5. **Codex and provenance system spec:** content schema, citation rules, UI.
6. **Generation spec:** the causal chain from star to planet to biome, with the statistics it must satisfy.
7. **UX:** onboarding, maps, HUD, photo mode, sharing.
8. **Production plan:** milestones, staffing, risk register.

## 16. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| WebGPU precision and depth issues at galactic scale | Spike first; keep a fallback renderer path |
| Data licensing blocks key imagery | Verify early; fall back to procedural textures |
| Scope creep (the Star Citizen failure mode) | Hard V1 cut line; "not in V1" list is binding |
| "Realistic" planets look boring | Invest in lighting, atmospheres, and scale cues; every body needs a notable feature |
| Scientific inaccuracy erodes trust | Provenance badges, citations, external reviewer |
| Procedural sameness | Causal generation; hand-authored anchor locations |
| Overpromising publicly | Announce only what ships (No Man's Sky lesson) |
| DR4 and other data updates change catalogs | Versioned pipeline with promotion logic |
